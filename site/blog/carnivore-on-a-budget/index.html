Carnivore on a budget: the cheap cuts that do the work.

Paula Wöbcke · 4 min read

Everyone pictures a ribeye. Your bank balance pictures a very different week. The good news is that the cuts that cost the least are the ones I reach for most, and they're the ones the Thermomix is best at looking after.

My four workhorses

Beef mince. The most forgiving thing in the fridge. Burgers, meatballs, a quick pan-fry with butter, a big batch of bolognese-style mince with no pasta in sight. Pick the fattier mince, because the fat is half the point.

Beef shin and other braising cuts. Tough and cheap, and unbeatable after a few hours of slow cooking. This is where the slow-cook mode earns its place on the bench.

Liver. Very cheap and not for everyone. If you're nervous, blitz a little into mince for burgers and see if anybody notices. Nobody has so far.

Butter and tallow. Buy butter in bulk when it's on offer. Render your own tallow from the fat you'd otherwise lose. It's free flavour.

"The ribeye is a treat. The shin is the plan." — Paula says

Where the Thermomix saves you money

Cheap cuts need time or technique, and that is the bit most of us skip when we're tired. So I let the Thermomix hold the time for me. Shin goes in with broth and walks itself to tender. Mince gets browned in the bowl, then left alone. Nothing sticks, nothing burns, and nothing gets thrown out because I forgot it.

Less waste is the quiet saving. When one Thermomix weighs, cooks and portions, you stop buying "just in case" and start buying what the week needs.

A simple budget week

Mince three nights, one big braise that covers two dinners and a lunch, eggs for breakfast, liver once if you're feeling brave. Butter on everything. It isn't glamorous, and it is properly filling.
